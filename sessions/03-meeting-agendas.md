# Block 3: Meeting agendas, and the five-part prompt

Status: draft
Time: 11:00–12:00 (60 min) · Talk 17 / do 43
Outcomes: LO2

## Purpose

Learners get one structure that fixes most weak prompts, and use it on the first
of their three target tasks: a meeting agenda with action items.

## The five parts

| Part | Question | Agenda example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Task** | What exactly do you want? | "Draft a 60-minute agenda for the kickoff of a data-quality working group" |
| **Context** | What facts can't it know? | Attendees, goals, open items from the last meeting, the email thread (pasted) |
| **Audience** | Who uses it, and what do they care about? | "Eight analysts from three divisions who haven't worked together; two are sceptical of the group" |
| **Format** | What shape, length, tone? | "Timed items adding up to 60 minutes; owner for each; decisions needed in bold; an action-item table (action, owner, due date) at the end" |
| **Guardrails** | What must it not do; what if it's unsure? | "Use only these facts. Don't invent attendees or topics. Write [CHECK] where something's missing" |

**[CHECK]** is the most useful guardrail in the course. Say so.

## Why test at the end of the relay

A prompt that only works on the scenario it was written for is an answer, not a
prompt. The swap test (T3.1) checks reusability; the empty-card test (T3.2)
checks whether the guardrails actually stop invention. Most teams' first prompts
fail T3.2, which is the best possible setup for Block 4.

## Why the Worst Prompt Contest works

To write a deliberately bad prompt, teams have to know what makes one good.
After the laughs, ask what made the winner so bad. The answers are the five
parts in reverse.

## Materials

- Activity 3 handout: relay sheets and three scenario cards (one per team)
- Prompt checklist on every table
- Scoreboard

## Check for understanding

By minute 45, every team's relay sheet shows all five parts, with a note on what
each changed. Watch for empty guardrails; it's the part people skip.

## Common problems

| Problem | Response |
| --- | --- |
| "Act as a world-class executive assistant…" | Not one of the five parts. Ask what facts or audience it replaces |
| One person types everything | That's why it's a relay: the keyboard moves with the sheet |
| The agenda has invented attendees | Perfect for the debrief: which guardrail would have stopped it? |

**Never cut the relay.** If you're behind, shorten the demo.

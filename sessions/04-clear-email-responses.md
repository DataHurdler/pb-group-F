# Block 4: Clear email responses, and iterating

Status: draft
Time: 13:00–14:00 (60 min) · Talk 15 / do 45
Outcomes: LO3

## Purpose

First drafts are rarely final. Learners practise getting from a weak output to a
good one with specific follow-ups, using their second target task: turning a
confusing email into a clear response.

## Outline

**Noise first.** It's straight after lunch, so nobody gets a lecture: a
volunteer reads a ridiculous email aloud.

| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0–3 | **Dramatic reading** | Volunteer | Hand the [Email from Chaos](../activities/4a-email-from-chaos.md) to a volunteer and ask for a full performance. The room counts on their fingers how many questions it actually asks |

Then two tests: the coverage check **T4.1**, and the final prompt on a different email, **T4.2**. Points for every ask answered, every unknown flagged, no invented commitments, and passing T4.2 |

## Five refinement moves (10 min)

Ask the room to name a fix they used before you name each move. Copy-paste
versions of all five are practice prompt **P4.3**; show the model prompt **P4.2**
at the end.

1. **Point at the problem.** "You left out the question about the template.
   Answer it in one sentence." Better than regenerating and hoping.
2. **Show an example.** Paste a reply you've written before that has the right
   tone. Examples beat adjectives.
3. **Ask it to ask you.** "Before drafting, ask me up to five questions about
   anything you need." Good when you don't know what's missing.
4. **Break it into steps.** "First list every question in the email. Then draft
   a reply that answers each one, numbered."
5. **Ask what it changed or assumed**, then check that list yourself.

And two ChatGPT mechanics worth showing on screen: **editing your earlier
message** (rewrites the conversation from that point) versus **adding a
follow-up** (keeps the history). Also when to **start a new chat**: when it keeps
repeating a mistake, or you're switching tasks.

## Materials

- Email from Chaos handout (the email, the facts you know, the log), one per
  learner
- Activity 4b: the three pre-made outputs (generated in prep), printed or on
  slides
- Scoreboard

## Check for understanding

Every pair's final reply answers all five asks, flags the unconfirmed deadline,
and doesn't commit to anything the facts don't support. Every log shows at least
one specific follow-up that fixed something.

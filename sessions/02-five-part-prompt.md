# 02 The five-part prompt

Status: draft
Time: 11:00–12:00 (60 min) · Talk 20 / do 40
Outcomes: LO2

## Purpose

Learners get one simple, memorable structure that fixes most weak prompts, and
use it on three ordinary federal tasks.

## By the end of this session, learners can…

- Write a prompt with all five parts (task, context, audience, format,
  guardrails) for a routine work task, and explain what each part changed in the
  output (LO2).

## The five parts

On the job aid: [../materials/handouts/prompt-checklist.md](../materials/handouts/prompt-checklist.md).

| Part | Question it answers | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Task** | What do you want produced? | "Draft an email announcement…" |
| **Context** | What facts does it need, which it can't know? | "The new schedule starts March 2. Staff may telework up to two days a week…" (paste the source) |
| **Audience** | Who reads it, and what do they care about? | "Frontline staff in a regional office, many of whom asked about childcare days" |
| **Format** | What shape, length, and tone? | "Under 150 words, plain language, three bullets for the key changes, friendly but official" |
| **Guardrails** | What must it not do, and what should it do when unsure? | "Use only the facts above. Don't invent dates or rules. If something is missing, write [CHECK] instead of guessing" |

The guardrail **"if something is missing, write [CHECK]"** is the most useful
single line in the course. Say so.

## Outline

| Min | Segment | Format | What happens |

| 0–5 | Hook | Room | Two announcements side by side: this morning's vague-prompt output, and one from a five-part prompt. *What did the second prompt say that the first didn't?* Collect answers; they'll name most of the five parts themselves |

## Demo script: the telework announcement

Source facts (fabricated) for the demo, on a slide:

> New telework schedule for the Regional Operations Division, effective March 2.
> Eligible staff may telework up to two days per week. Telework days must be
> agreed with your supervisor by February 20. Core in-office days are Tuesday and
> Thursday. Questions: Lee Okafor, Workforce Programs.

Run in this order, saying what changed each time:

1. *Write an announcement about the new telework schedule.* (Invents everything.)
2. Add task and context: paste the facts. (Accurate, but generic and long.)
3. Add format: under 150 words, three bullets. (Better shape, still stiff.)
4. Add audience: frontline staff who have asked about flexibility. (Tone shifts.)
5. Add guardrails: only these facts; [CHECK] for gaps. (Watch for [CHECK]; the
   facts don't say whether part-time staff are eligible.)

Never promise the room a specific output. If a run surprises you, say so and use
it.

## Check for understanding

By minute 45, every pair has at least two rewritten prompts with all five parts
visible. Look for missing guardrails; it's the part people skip.

## Common problems

| Problem | Response |
| --- | --- |
| Prompts get very long | Long is fine if every line earns its place. Ask: what would go wrong if you removed this line? |
| "Act as an expert…" openers | Harmless, but not one of the five. Ask what facts or audience it replaces |
| One person types everything | Swap at the second prompt |

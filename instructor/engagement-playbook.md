# Engagement playbook: no death by PowerPoint

How the day stays active, what you need for the games, and spare ideas for when
the room needs a lift.

## The rules

1. **The screen shows ChatGPT, not slides,** for most of the day. Live outputs
   are more interesting than bullets, and they're never quite what you expect.
2. **Slide budget: about 20 slides for the whole day.** Title, agenda, the five
   parts, the five refinement moves, game instructions, and scoring. If you're
   making slide 21, turn it into a handout or a question instead.
3. **No talk segment over 12 minutes.** The longest is the failure-modes talk in
   Block 2, and it asks the room a question after every row.
4. **Every block opens with learners doing something:** voting, writing, or
   performing. Never with objectives on a slide.
5. **Every game has a point, and the debrief says it out loud.** Fun without the
   debrief is just a break.
6. **Read the prompt, not the output** in share-outs. It keeps attention on the
   thing that transfers.

## What happens when

| Block | Game | It teaches |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | **Human or Machine?** Spot the ChatGPT agenda | Vague prompts get generic output |
| 1 | **Same prompt, different answers** | Outputs vary; gaps get filled with inventions |
| 2 | **Hallucination Hunt**, with points and penalties | Check against the source; don't over-accuse either |
| 2 | **Machine vs. Hunters**: ChatGPT checks the same paper and joins the scoreboard | A check prompt is a second pair of eyes, not a substitute |
| 2 | **Two Truths and an AI**, lightning rounds | The fabrication is the most plausible-sounding line |
| 2 | **"Are you sure?"** demo, with the room predicting | Leading questions get agreement; "are you sure?" isn't checking |
| 3 | **Worst Prompt Contest** | What a good prompt needs, learned by its absence |
| 3 | **Prompt Makeover Relay**, ending with the swap-card and empty-card tests | What each of the five parts does, and whether the prompt survives a new input |
| 4 | **Email from Chaos**, read aloud with feeling | Iterating with specific follow-ups |
| 4 | **Reverse-Engineer the Prompt** | Which prompt features cause which output features |
| 5 | **Red Team** | ChatGPT's critiques need your judgement too |
| 5 | **Elevator Briefing** to the "Deputy Director" | Talking points are a draft; you're the one briefing |
| 6 | **Partner test**: your partner tries to break your prompt with a messy input | Testing finds what you'd never notice yourself |
| 6 | **Gallery walk** with sticky dots | Learning from each other's prompts and test logs |

## The scoreboard

A flip chart on the wall with a column per team, **plus a column for ChatGPT**
after Machine vs. Hunters, updated after every game. Keep
it light: the scores matter less than the debrief. Announce the winner at 16:23.

Prizes should be nominal: stickers, a printed "Prompt Champion" certificate, or
first pick of the leftover snacks. [TODO: check whether the host agency has

## Spare ideas

Use these if you have time, or to replace something that isn't landing.

- **Four Corners** (3 min, after a break). Label the corners *Always*, *With
  checking*, *Rarely*, *Never*. Read a scenario ("Use ChatGPT to draft a reply
  to a congressional inquiry"; "…to decide which contractor wins"; "…to
  summarise a public report") and people walk to their answer. Ask one person
  per corner why.
- **Prompt Golf** (8 min, fast teams in Block 4). Get an output that meets five
  criteria in the fewest words. Then discuss: which words carried the weight?
- **Difficult stakeholder role-play.** ChatGPT plays a frustrated counterpart in
  another agency; a learner practises a reply, then asks ChatGPT for feedback on
  their tone.
- **Prompt clinic.** A learner volunteers a prompt that isn't working (invented
  content); the room fixes it live, one part at a time.
- **Exit line.** In 15 words or fewer: what will you do differently on Monday?
  Read a few aloud.

## If the course runs virtually

| In person | Virtual |
| --- | --- |
| A/B/C cards | Poll, or type the letter in chat on "3, 2, 1" |
| Tables | Breakout rooms of 3–4 with a shared document |
| Relay sheet | Shared document; the "keyboard" is screen-sharing, which rotates |
| Elevator Briefing | Briefer on camera; everyone else muted |
| Gallery walk | Cards pasted into a shared document; reactions instead of dots |
| Four Corners | Poll with the four options |

## Four rules

1. **Use only tools your agency has approved, and only for the information they're
   approved for.** A tool being available on your computer doesn't mean every
   kind of information may go into it.
2. **Don't paste sensitive information into a tool that isn't approved for it.**
   That includes:
   - Personally identifiable information (names linked to SSNs, medical, financial,
     or personnel details)
   - Controlled Unclassified Information (CUI)
   - Classified information, which never goes into these tools
   - Pre-decisional, deliberative, procurement-sensitive, or law-enforcement
     information
3. **You're accountable for what you use.** Check every fact, figure, date, and
   citation against a real source before it goes anywhere. "The AI wrote it"
   isn't a defence.
4. **Prompts and outputs can be federal records.** Follow your agency's guidance
   on keeping them, and on disclosing AI use where it requires that.

## Today's room rule

Everything you paste today is **fabricated or public**. Nothing from your real
work goes in.

## Stop and ask first if…

- you're unsure whether the information is sensitive
- the output will be used in a decision about a person: hiring, performance,
  benefits, eligibility, enforcement
- the output goes to the public, Congress, or the press without another person
  reviewing it
- you'd be uncomfortable if your use of AI on this task were made public

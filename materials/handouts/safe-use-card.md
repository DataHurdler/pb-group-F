## Today you're using the public version of ChatGPT

It's the same ChatGPT anyone can sign up for, not a government or enterprise
version. That makes the main rule simple:

**Only invented or public information goes in.**

## Four rules

1. **Nothing non-public from your work goes into public ChatGPT.** That
   includes:
   - Personally identifiable information (SSNs, home addresses, personnel
     actions)
   - Controlled Unclassified Information (CUI)
   - Classified information
   - Pre-decisional or deliberative material: unannounced reorganisations,
     draft budgets, draft positions
   - Procurement-sensitive information: vendor quotes, source-selection details
   - Internal emails, meeting notes, and documents, even ones that seem harmless
2. **Check your data controls.** Conversations in the public version may be used
   to train models unless you change the setting. [TODO: the setting the agency
   wants learners to choose.]
3. **You're accountable for what you use.** Check every number, source, date, and
   claim against the original. "ChatGPT wrote it" isn't a defence.
4. **For real work, use only what your agency approves.** Find out whether it has
   an approved AI tool, what that tool is approved for, and what its policy says
   about public tools.

## Stop and ask first if…

- you're not sure whether information is public
- the output would feed a decision about a person, a contract, or an enforcement
  action
- the output would go to Congress, the press, or the public without another
  person reviewing it

## Four rules

1. **Use the version of ChatGPT your agency provides, for the information it's
   approved for.** Not a personal account, unless your agency says that's
   allowed. Being logged in doesn't mean every kind of information may go in.
2. **Don't paste sensitive information into a version that isn't approved for
   it.** That includes:
   - Personally identifiable information (SSNs, home addresses, personnel
     actions)
   - Controlled Unclassified Information (CUI)
   - Classified information, which never goes into ChatGPT
   - Pre-decisional or deliberative material: unannounced reorganisations,
     draft budgets, draft positions
   - Procurement-sensitive information: vendor quotes, source-selection details
3. **You're accountable for what you use.** Check every number, source, date, and
   claim against the original before it goes anywhere. "ChatGPT wrote it" isn't
   a defence.
4. **Prompts and outputs can be federal records.** Follow your agency's guidance
   on keeping them and on disclosing AI use.

## Today's room rule

Everything you paste today is **invented or public**. Nothing from your real work
goes in.

## Stop and ask first if…

- you're not sure whether information is sensitive
- the output feeds a decision about a person, a contract, or an enforcement action
- the output goes to Congress, the press, or the public without another person
  reviewing it
- you'd be uncomfortable if your use of ChatGPT on this task became public

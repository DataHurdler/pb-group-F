# Activity 3: Worst Prompt Contest, and Prompt Makeover Relay

Block 3 · Outcome: LO2 · Time: 8 + 25 min · Format: teams of 3–4

## For the instructor

### Part 1: Worst Prompt Contest (8 min)

**Why:** writing a deliberately terrible prompt requires knowing what a good one
has. It's also the loudest five minutes of the morning.

| Min | Step |
| --- | --- |
| 0–3 | "Write the worst possible prompt for getting a useful meeting agenda out of ChatGPT. It must be a real attempt to get an agenda, not just gibberish" |
| 3–6 | Each team reads theirs aloud. Room votes by applause |
| 6–8 | Run the winner live. Ask: *what made it so bad?* Write the answers on the board; they're the five parts, inverted |

Typical winners: "agenda pls"; "make the meeting good"; a 200-word rant with no
facts in it.

### Part 2: Prompt Makeover Relay (25 min)

**Why:** each person adds one part and sees its effect, so the team learns what
each part *does*, not just that it exists.

**Setup:** one relay sheet and one scenario card per team. Give different teams
different scenarios so the debrief has variety.

| Min | Step |
| --- | --- |
| 0–2 | Rules: the sheet and keyboard move together. Each person adds one part, runs the full prompt in a **new chat**, and writes one line on what changed |
| 2–17 | Relay. With three people, someone takes two parts |
| 17–25 | **Test the final prompt:** T3.1 (swap scenario cards with the next team, paste their facts into your prompt, change nothing else) and T3.2 (the near-empty card). Score each against the pass criteria on the relay sheet |

**Scoring:** 3 points for a sheet with all five parts and a "what changed" note
for each; +1 for passing T3.1; +2 for passing T3.2 (the one most prompts fail).

Prompts and pass criteria: [../prompts/block-3.md](../prompts/block-3.md).

**Watch for:** context that's summarised instead of pasted; guardrails left
blank; agendas whose times don't add up (a good catch to praise).

## For learners

### Relay sheet

Start with this prompt: ***"Make an agenda for my meeting."***

| Turn | Part you add | What you added (short) | What changed in the output |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | **Task**: exactly what you want | | |
| 2 | **Context**: facts from the scenario card, pasted | | |
| 3 | **Audience**: who'll use it, what they care about | | |
| 4 | **Format**: length, structure, timing, action-item table | | |
| 5 | **Guardrails**: what it must not do; [CHECK] for gaps | | |

**Final check:** compare the last agenda with your scenario card. Is anything
there that the card doesn't support?

### Test your final prompt

Pass criteria for both tests: times add up to the meeting length · every item has
an owner or [CHECK] · an action-item table · nothing invented.

| Test | What you do | Pass? | What failed |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **T3.1 Swap the card** | Paste the next team's scenario facts into your prompt. Change nothing else. New chat | | |
| **T3.2 Empty card** | Replace the facts with: *"30-minute meeting on Thursday about the dashboard."* New chat | | |

On T3.2, a good prompt gives you [CHECK]s or questions. A weak one invents a
whole meeting.

### Scenario cards

All names and details are fabricated.

**Card 1: Working group kickoff**
> 60 minutes, Tuesday Nov 3, 14:00, hybrid. Eight analysts from Policy Analysis,
> Economic Research, and Program Evaluation; most haven't worked together. Goals:
> agree a one-page charter, choose two co-chairs, set a monthly meeting time.
> Open questions from the email thread: is the group advisory or decision-making?
> Who maintains the shared data dictionary? Two members have said they doubt the
> group is needed.

**Card 2: The status meeting that always runs over**
> Weekly, 30 minutes, hard stop. Five workstreams: survey redesign (on track),
> dashboard refresh (on track), FY27 data call (blocked: waiting on template
> approval from Legal), records clean-up (blocked: two staff out), report
> templates (on track). Last week's open action items: J. Park to send revised
> survey to OMB liaison (due Oct 30); M. Ruiz to confirm dashboard go-live date
> (no date given).

**Card 3: Pre-brief before a Deputy Director briefing**
> 45 minutes, prepping a 15-minute briefing to Deputy Director Alvarez on the
> helpdesk survey results (from this morning's Hunt). Attendees: the analyst who
> wrote the paper, the helpdesk lead, and the division chief. Need to agree the
> three key messages, who presents, and answers to likely questions. The
> division chief wants to mention the new ticketing system; the analyst thinks
> the data doesn't support a causal claim.

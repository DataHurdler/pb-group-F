# Activity 2.1: Fix three prompts

Session: 02 · Outcome: LO2 · Time: 23 min · Format: pairs

## For the instructor

**Why this activity:** learners apply all five parts to three different kinds of
task (a reply to the public, an explanation for a new employee, a plan), and see
the difference each makes.

**Setup:** handout and job aid on every desk. Pairs share one device or use two.

**Timing**

| Min | Step |
| --- | --- |
| 0–2 | Brief: run the vague version first, then your rewrite. Do them in order; prompt 3 is a stretch |
| 2–23 | Pairs work; circulate. Note one strong prompt to show in the debrief |

**What good looks like (prompt 1)**

> Draft a reply to the email below from a member of the public asking about their
> grant application. **Context:** our current processing time is 10–12 weeks from
> the submission date; we can't discuss application details by email; applicants
> can check status at the online portal under "My Applications." **Audience:** a
> small-business owner who's frustrated and may not know how federal grants work.
> **Format:** under 120 words, plain language, warm but professional, no jargon.
> **Guardrails:** don't promise a decision date or outcome. Use only the facts
> above. If you need a fact I haven't given, write [CHECK].
>
> [pasted email]

**Likely mistakes:**
- Context left out because "it's obvious." Ask: obvious to whom?
- No guardrails, so the reply promises a date. Point to the output.
- For prompt 2, the steps get rearranged or merged. Ask them to compare against
  the four steps.

**If a pair finishes early:** take prompt 1 and change only the audience (say, a
member of Congress's caseworker instead of the applicant). What changes?

---

## For learners

**Your task:** for each prompt below, run the vague version, then rewrite it with
all five parts (task, context, audience, format, guardrails) using the job aid,
and run your version. Use only the facts given. All are fabricated.

### Prompt 1: reply to the public

**Vague version:** *Write a reply to this email.*

> Hello, I submitted my small-business grant application eight weeks ago and the
> status still says "Received." Has anyone even looked at it? I need to know
> whether to hire for the project. — Sam Rivera

**Facts you can use:** current processing time is 10–12 weeks from submission.
Staff can't discuss application details by email. Status is in the online
portal under "My Applications."

### Prompt 2: explain a process to a new employee

**Vague version:** *Explain how to do a travel voucher.*

**Facts you can use:** in your office, vouchers are due within 5 business days of
returning. Steps: (1) upload receipts for lodging and any expense over $75;
(2) complete the voucher in the travel system and match it to the approved
authorization; (3) sign and route it to your supervisor; (4) correct and
resubmit within 3 business days if it's returned.

### Prompt 3 (stretch): a plan

**Vague version:** *Make a training plan.*

**Facts you can use:** a team of eight is moving to a new case-tracking system on
November 16. You have two hours of training time per person before then. Comfort
with technology varies widely; two people have used the system at another agency.

### Compare

For each prompt: what did the vague version invent or get wrong? Which of the
five parts made the biggest difference?

**Time:** 21 minutes

# Activity 5: Strip, Build, Red Team, Brief

Block 5 · Outcomes: LO2, LO3, LO4 · Time: 55 min · Format: teams of 3–4

## For the instructor

**Why this activity:** it's the most realistic task of the day. The source
material contains things that shouldn't go into ChatGPT; the task is big enough
to need decomposition; the critique step needs judgement; and the briefing is
what these learners actually do with a plan.

**Timing:** see [../sessions/05-implementation-checklists.md](../sessions/05-implementation-checklists.md).

**Strip step: what teams should catch**

| Item | Why it shouldn't be pasted | Points |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The vendor's quote, **$48,500**, marked procurement-sensitive | Procurement-sensitive information | +2 with a reason |
| The **possible merger** of Program Evaluation into Economic Research, marked pre-decisional | Pre-decisional and unannounced | +2 with a reason |

Neither is needed for the checklist anyway, which is a useful point in itself:
*if it isn't needed for the task, it doesn't go in.* Whether either *could* go
into the learners' version of ChatGPT depends on what the agency has approved it
for. Say so; don't guess.

**What a strong checklist has:** phases in a sensible order (design, build,
test, train, launch, retire the old inbox); an owner and date on every task, or
[CHECK]; dependencies (training can't happen before the form is configured);
nothing invented, such as staff names or dates not in the summary.

**Red-team critiques to expect:** some sharp (no testing phase; training the
week before launch; no plan for requests in flight when the inbox closes), some
generic ("communication is key"), some wrong (assuming a budget problem the
summary doesn't mention). A team earns +2 for rejecting a weak critique with a
reason.

**Elevator Briefing points:** +2 for the clearest briefing, voted by applause.
+1 for anyone who answers your hard question with "I'll get back to you on
that" rather than bluffing.

## For learners

### The project summary

All names, figures, and details are fabricated.

> **New data-request intake form: rollout**
>
> Replace the shared email inbox for data requests with a single online intake
> form, across three divisions (Policy Analysis, Economic Research, Program
> Evaluation), by **Monday, January 11, 2027**.
>
> - Form fields to be finalised by A. Nguyen (Policy Analysis) by Nov 20.
> - IT to configure the form on the agency's SharePoint site. Vendor support
>   from Brightline Consulting; **quote received: $48,500 (procurement
>   sensitive, do not distribute)**.
> - About 45 staff need training before launch.
> - The standard operating procedure for data requests needs updating.
> - The old inbox to be retired two weeks after launch.
> - **Pre-decisional, not for distribution:** Program Evaluation may be merged
>   into Economic Research in FY2027. Not yet announced.

### Step 1: Strip (5 min)

**Before you paste anything**, highlight every item that shouldn't go into
ChatGPT. Write why next to it.

### Step 2: Build (15 min)

Use the five parts. Break the task into steps in your prompt:

1. List the phases of the rollout.
2. For each phase, list tasks with owner, due date, and dependency.
3. Produce the checklist as a table: ☐ · Task · Owner · Due · Depends on.
   Write [CHECK] for any owner or date the summary doesn't give.

### Step 3: Red Team (13 min)

Paste your checklist into ChatGPT with this prompt:

> You are a sceptical division chief who has watched three rollouts like this
> fail. List the five most likely reasons this plan will fail, most serious
> first. Be specific to this checklist.

For each critique, decide: **accept** (and change the checklist) or **reject**
(and say why).

### Step 4: Elevator Briefing (60 seconds per team)

Ask ChatGPT for **60-second talking points** for briefing the Deputy Director on
this plan, bottom line first. Edit them. Your briefer then stands up and briefs
the instructor, who is playing the Deputy Director and will ask one hard
question.

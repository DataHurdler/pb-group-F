## 2. Decided

- **Six hours of instruction**, plus a **one-hour lunch** and **four 15-minute
  breaks** (two in the morning, two in the afternoon): 8:30–16:30.
- **Tool: the public version of ChatGPT, running GPT-6**, not a government or
  enterprise version. This makes the data rule simple and strict: **only
  invented or public information goes in.** Every activity and prompt in this
  repo already meets that rule.
- **Practice and test prompts are built into every block.** A
  [prompt bank](prompts/README.md) holds every prompt the course uses, ready to
  copy, plus test inputs and a ten-prompt take-home set. Each of Blocks 2–6 ends
  its main activity by testing the output or the prompt.
- **Target tasks:** a meeting agenda with action items, a project implementation
  checklist, and turning a confusing email into a clear response. Research,
  analysis, report writing, and briefings supply the context.
- **Out:** grant writing and medical information.

## 3. Assumptions

- In person, one instructor, 12–24 learners in teams of 3–4, each with a laptop
  that can reach chatgpt.com.
- No formal assessment; the pre/post check and prompt cards are for learning and
  improving the course.

## 5. Audience

- **Who:** US federal employees with at least a bachelor's degree, in roles
  centred on research, analysis, report writing, and briefings: policy and
  program analysts, researchers, evaluators, and their supervisors.
- **Already know:** their subject matter; how to write and structure reports,
  memos, and briefings; BLUF; how to weigh evidence.
- **Don't assume they know:** how LLMs work; a structured way to prompt; how to
  test a prompt; which information may go into a public AI tool.
- **What they want:** faster, better first drafts of routine work, especially
  agendas, checklists, and email replies.
- **Likely worries:** *Am I allowed? Will it make things up in my analysis? Is
  this worth the time?* As analysts, they'll be quick to spot weak evidence; the
  Hunt and the test prompts are built for that.

## 6. Scope

**In:** how ChatGPT generates text; writing, refining, testing, and reusing
prompts for agendas, checklists, and email; checking outputs, especially
numbers, sources, and claims of cause; data-handling basics for a public tool;
accountability.

**Out, and where to send people who ask:**

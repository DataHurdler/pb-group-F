# Pre/post check

Status: draft. Five questions, about five minutes. Taken at 8:56 and again at
16:11, with **the same questions both times**.

Anonymous and not graded. Say so, and that "I don't know" is a good morning
answer. To compare the two attempts anonymously, learners write the same
made-up code word on both.

---

**1. You send ChatGPT the exact same prompt twice and get two noticeably
different answers. Why?** (LO1)

- A. Something is wrong with ChatGPT
- B. It generates text by predicting likely words, with some built-in variation
- C. It learned from your first prompt and deliberately changed its answer
- D. I don't know

**2. ChatGPT gives you a confident paragraph that includes a statistic and names
a published report as its source. What's the best next step?** (LO1, LO4)

- A. Use it; naming a source shows it's reliable
- B. Ask ChatGPT "Are you sure?"
- C. Find the report yourself and check the statistic
- D. I don't know

**3. Your agency's version of ChatGPT is approved for public information only.
Which of these can you paste in?** (LO4)

- A. A draft memo describing an unannounced reorganisation
- B. A report already published on your agency's public website
- C. A vendor's price quote for an upcoming contract
- D. I don't know

**4. ChatGPT's reply to a confusing email is accurate but skips one of the
sender's questions. What's the best next move?** (LO3)

- A. Regenerate the response and hope it's included this time
- B. Follow up: "You didn't answer the question about the template. Add one
  sentence answering it"
- C. Start over in a new chat with the same prompt
- D. I don't know

**5. Which prompt is most likely to give you a usable meeting agenda?** (LO2)

- A. "Make an agenda for my meeting."
- B. "You are a world-class executive assistant. Create an excellent agenda."
- C. "Draft a 30-minute agenda for our weekly status meeting with five
  workstream leads. Two items are blocked: [details]. Timed items adding up to
  30 minutes, an owner for each, and an action-item table at the end. Use only
  these facts; write [CHECK] for anything missing."
- D. I don't know

---

## Answers

| Q | Answer | A wrong answer suggests |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | B | C: thinks ChatGPT learns permanently from each user. Block 1 talk |
| 2 | C | A or B: trusts fluency or self-checking. The point of Block 2 |
| 3 | B | A or C: doesn't yet see pre-decisional or procurement information as sensitive. Block 5 strip step |
| 4 | B | A or C: hasn't learned to point at the problem. Block 4 |
| 5 | C | B: believes role-prompt folklore over context. Block 3 |

## Using the results

- **Morning:** if most of the room gets Q3 wrong, spend extra time on the
  safe-use card.
- **Afternoon:** tell the room how the scores moved.
- **Afterwards:** a question nobody improved on points at the block to fix.
